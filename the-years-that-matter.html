Insight

The years that matter most are the ones most people miss.

← All Insights

A tragedy of personal finance is time lost. The years when saving and investing can have their most profound effect are the years when people feel least able to do it. When we're young, life seems endless and money is usually in short supply. As we age, responsibilities multiply. Somewhere in our forties and fifties, we start investing more earnestly, but by then, we've surrendered the most powerful asset we ever had: the years when compounding was quietly doing its most important work.

For those who did save through those earlier years, who watched their spending, avoided debt, and stayed disciplined, something remarkable happens in their targeted retirement year. Their investments provide more than just enough. Retirement becomes a choice, not a rescue operation.

The goal is not to cross a finish line. It is to build something that gives you choices.

- Duane Johnson, ChFC® CLU®
